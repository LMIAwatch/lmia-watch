Status lifecycle

Each posting or list row is one record. An employer's status is the most recent record's status.

StatusAssigned whenEnds when
PendingA Job Bank posting carries the "LMIA requested" flag in the latest scrapeThe flag changes to approved, the employer appears in a quarterly list, or the posting closes with no decision seen
ApprovedThe Job Bank flag reads "Approved LMIA", or the employer, city and occupation appear in a positive quarterly listNever. Approved records stay on the profile with their quarter.
RefusedThe employer, city and occupation appear in a negative quarterly listNever
LapsedA pending posting closed and two full quarterly lists have been published since, with no matching row in eitherA later list matches it
FindingThe employer appears on IRCC's non-compliant listNever. Shown separately from the statuses above because it is a government decision, not a pattern.

Indicators

Each indicator is a mechanical rule over the records. A triggered indicator means the pattern is present. It does not mean the employer did anything wrong, and every rule has known false positives, listed here.

Rules are versioned. A change to any rule recomputes every profile and is logged on the Data page.

Matching employers across sources

Names are normalized before comparison: case folded, punctuation removed, legal suffixes stripped (Ltd., Inc., Corp., ULC, Limited Partnership), and accents preserved. A match is then graded:

GradeConditionShown as
ExactNormalized name and full postal code agreeMerged into one profile
ProbableNormalized name and city agree, or name only where the posting uses a trade nameMerged, with "Probable" on the registry card and both raw strings visible
PossibleName similarity above a threshold, different cityListed as "possibly related", never merged
UnmatchedNothing above thresholdOwn profile with no registry card

Exclusions

Private households and individuals are not published. Job Bank pending postings include caregiver and home-support jobs where the employer is a person; those postings are dropped before anything is stored. ESDC already removes employers with personal names from its lists, so the site's coverage of caregiver hiring is incomplete by design.

No worker is ever named. Applicant counts, resumes and contact details on a posting are not collected.

Limits

The ESDC lists count positions on LMIAs, not work permits issued or workers who arrived. The pending flag exists only for employers who advertised on Job Bank, which most streams require. Street addresses appear only when an employer entered one. The quarterly lists give a city and postal code. Registry matches are by name and can be wrong, which is why the raw strings and the grade are shown.
